# Fork and Derivative Policy

## Code rights

Forks and derivatives may exercise the rights granted by the applicable open-source licenses.

## Independent identity

An independently maintained fork, hosted implementation, issuer, or service should use a distinct primary identity and must not create a misleading impression of official ARV operation, endorsement, certification, or authorization.

An accurate secondary statement such as `independent fork of ARV Proof OSS` may be used when it is truthful and non-confusing.

## Trust separation

Independent deployments should operate their own:

- signing keys;
- issuer identifiers;
- trust registries or keyrings;
- service domains and endpoints;
- governance and authorization records.

No fork should ship or claim control of an ARV private signing key.

## Verification language

A derivative may accurately report cryptographic results under the key and policy actually evaluated.

It must not transform that result into an unsupported claim of material truth, official issuance, institutional endorsement, or legal certification.

## Compatibility

Compatibility statements should identify the public protocol, schema, or version actually tested.

This policy governs identity and official-status representations. It does not remove open-source code permissions.
